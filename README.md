# Bible Timeline

An interactive, visual Bible timeline for personal and church study. Explore the whole biblical story from Creation to Revelation: when things happened, who was involved, and where. Two linked views, a **Timeline** and a **Map**, share the same data and the same "current moment in time."

## What's in it

The whole Bible, Genesis to Revelation, live at [timeline.rector.app](https://timeline.rector.app):

- **236 events, 166 people, 142 places and 13 eras**, every chapter of Scripture cited by some event (bar a few genealogies and lists of laws), and 20 journeys drawn stop by stop.
- A vertical timeline (earliest at the top) that zooms from millennia down to individual days, with lanes for the main figures, kings, prophets, the church and world history.
- A time-aware map showing the nearest events, their places and journeys, and the empires and Israel's own kingdoms as they stood at that moment.
- One shared "current moment" and selection drive both views, shown side by side or one at a time; a family tree from Adam to Jesus can take the map's place.
- Detail panels for events, people, places, eras and empires, with the Scripture text (Berean Standard Bible) built in.
- Eleven guided tours from Creation to Paul, filters by type, person, book or passage, era and place, and shareable links.
- Installable on a phone or computer, and it works offline once loaded (the map only where you've already looked).
- Disputed dates (early vs. late Exodus, chosen in Settings) and disputed sites (Mount Sinai, the Red Sea crossing) carry alternates and notes.

## Getting started

Built with React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS and MapLibre GL. It's a static site: all content lives as JSON/GeoJSON in `src/data/`.

Requires [Node.js](https://nodejs.org/) 22 or later.

```sh
npm install      # once
npm run dev      # start the dev server at http://localhost:5173
npm test         # run tests (date math + data integrity)
npm run build    # type-check and build static files into dist/
```

## Deployment

Cloudflare Workers serves the built `dist` folder as static files (`wrangler.jsonc`), building from GitHub on every push: build command `npm test && npm run build`, deploy command `npx wrangler deploy`, Node version from `.node-version`. A failing test blocks the deploy. `main` is the public site at [timeline.rector.app](https://timeline.rector.app). Every other pushed branch gets its own preview URL, which Cloudflare Access keeps private (the account-wide Access switch for Workers).

## Content

| File | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `src/data/eras.json` | Eras with colors and date ranges |
| `src/data/empires.json` | The great powers for the World history lane, with their dates and capitals |
| `src/data/events.json` | Events with dates, people, places, regions, journey and Scripture |
| `src/data/people.json` | People with lifespans and family links |
| `src/data/genealogy.json` | Names from the genealogies (Seth, Obed, Luke's Heli) that link the family tree but get no person entry |
| `src/data/locations.json` | Places with coordinates, modern names and alternate sites |
| `src/data/journeys.json` | Ordered routes between places, with optional stop dates |
| `src/data/regions.json` | GeoJSON outlines of lands and regions (approximate) |
| `src/data/borders/` | Empires' and Israel's outlines over time: `cliopatria.json` (generated) and `drawn.json` (by hand) |
| `src/data/bible/` | The Berean Standard Bible text, one JSON per book (generated by `scripts/bsb.mjs`) |
| `src/data/tours.json` | Guided tours: steps that each select an event, with narration |

Conventions:

- **Years are astronomical:** 1 BC is `0`, 1446 BC is `-1445`, and AD 30 is `30`. Add `month` and `day` (approximate Julian placement) when Scripture gives them, plus a `label` for the biblical form, e.g. `"14 Nisan"`.
- **Mark key events `"major": true`.** When the timeline is crowded, their labels and dots show first. Keep it to what a reader would look for: the turning points, and the stories lay readers know best even when history weighs them less (Daniel in the lions' den beside Cyrus's decree). About one event in three at most.
- **Events within a larger event** (the Red Sea crossing during the Exodus) get `"partOf": "<parent id>"`. Keep them as their own events, with their own type, people and Scripture. Their date must fall within the parent's, and if the parent has a journey, one of their places must be a stop on it. Only one level deep.
- **Add a person** when they appear in two or more events, or a book is named after them or written by them, or they're the central figure of a story the timeline tells (Noah, Gideon, Ruth), or a parent the family tree needs with dates (Terah). Give them `dates` only when Scripture supports a lifespan, or for a reign or ministry it places (say which in `dateNote`); otherwise their bar spans their events. Put kings and rulers, prophets, and apostles and church figures in their `lane` (`kings`, `prophets`, `church`); the main figures stay in the default People lane, and list any other lane they fit in `alsoIn` (Peter is also `church`, David also `kings`). Someone in two lanes shows once, in the first that's on, except that kings always show in the Kings lane too, as their reign. Every king of Israel and Judah has an entry with its `kingdom`, and, when it differs from `dates` (a lifespan, or a reign counted from a coregency), its sole reign in `reign` (Thiele). Foreign rulers who act on Israel or the church get an entry too, dated by their reign.
- **Births and deaths** list the person they're about first, and must match that person's lifespan (`npm test` checks). Mark the ones the story dwells on `"major": true`; the rest are drawn as marks on the person's bar.
- **Dates are keyed by chronology:** `early` is the default and `late` holds late-Exodus alternates (readers choose in Settings). Anything without its own entry falls back to the default.
- **Tours** step through events in date order (`npm test` checks, in both chronologies). Each step's `text` is one or two sentences that tie the event to the story, rather than repeating its summary, which the details panel shows just below; quote Scripture with a reference where it helps.
- `npm test` checks that every file matches its schema and that every id it references exists.

Dates follow the early-Exodus chronology (1 Kings 6:1; Exodus 12:40) and the ages given in Genesis. Kings follow Thiele's chronology (the kingdom divides in 931 BC, Jerusalem falls in 586), and the crucifixion is dated AD 30, with AD 33 noted as the alternative. Prophets are dated by their ministry (event type `ministry`), letters by when and where they were written (`writing`). Genesis 1–11 is placed using the Masoretic genealogies but marked as "date unknown." Place coordinates follow [OpenBible.info's Bible geocoding data](https://www.openbible.info/geo/). Each location's `openbible` field names its OpenBible place (`"Bethel 1"`), with `#n` when we follow a candidate site other than OpenBible's first choice (`"Rephidim#2"`, the traditional site). `node scripts/openbible.mjs` reports places that drift from it or that OpenBible is unsure of; `--write` moves drifted points, and `find`/`show` search its places. Region outlines are still hand-drawn approximations. The empires on the map come from [Cliopatria](https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria): `node scripts/cliopatria.mjs` cuts its outlines to our empires' dates and simplifies them into `src/data/borders/cliopatria.json` (don't edit that file by hand); Israel's own outlines (the tribes, the kingdoms, Yehud, Herod's sons) are drawn by hand in `src/data/borders/drawn.json`, each with a note on how sure it is.
